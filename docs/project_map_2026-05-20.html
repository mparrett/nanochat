01 / The originating question

"Does Hope/NL help LM at our scale?"

The fork opened on 2026-04-29 with a ticket from the sibling trx4mr project: prototype Hope / Nested Learning (Behrouz et al., arXiv:2512.24695) on top of nanochat's tested CPU/MPS pipeline rather than rebuilding from scratch. The architectural bet: a conventional transformer block is stateless — output = f(x, params). Hope makes blocks carry input-driven, per-token mutable state — output, state = f(x, params, state). The block returns an updated memory.

The originating equation we'd start from is from §2 of the paper: 

M_t = α_t · M_{t-1} + v_t · k_t^T   (rank-1 fast-weight update)
y_t = M_t · q_t                      (read)

nanochat was chosen over picoGPT because it already had a working SFT + ChatCORE + RL pipeline on Apple Silicon, and because its single-complexity-dial discipline (--depth derives all other hyperparameters) is exactly the right constraint for an architectural-question experiment: any change has to work across depths, not just at one scale.

The strategic-plan staging (Stages 0-6) came from the trx4mr ticket. Stage 0 is plumbing (return None for memory_state, no behavior change). Stage 1 swaps the FFN in one block for fixed-α/η linear-attention memory. Stage 2 makes α and η per-token learned gates. Stages 3-6 generalize to chained memory blocks (CMS), the full Hope architecture, and the M3 optimizer. The originating bet was that Stage 2 or 3 would visibly help LM val_bpb at d6 on Apple Silicon.

02 / The build

Stages 0, 1, 2 — what got constructed

Three weeks of construction landed Stages 0-2. Each stage's discipline was that the synthetic MQAR probe had to clear baseline before any d6 pretrain budget was spent. This rule paid out immediately:

  • Stage 0 — memory_state plumbing through GPT.forward and Block.forward. 6 contract tests pin bit-identical loss/logits with and without state. Commit d2bbc0e.
  • Stage 1 — LinearAttentionMemory module, swapped at one block via hope_memory_layer. Full d6 pretrain landed at val_bpb 1.179 vs baseline 1.174 — within seed noise. Commit bc54858.
  • Stage 1.5 — the MQAR probe (Multi-Query Associative Recall) became the gate. Stage 1's swap was 2× slower to grok than baseline on recall. Followup found the cause: W_o = 0 initialization creates a K/V/Q gradient-gating cold start. Fixing the Wo init scale to 1.0 closed the gap at step-by-step level. Commits 3f54bc6, 07e3312. This was the project's first architectural lesson: probe-first methodology surfaced a real cold-start trap that LM val_bpb couldn't have isolated.
  • Stage 2 — LearnedGateLinearMemory: per-token αt and ηt as sigmoid projections of xt, vectorized via prefix log-products so the cost class is the same O(T²) as Stage 1 with no Python loop. Optimizer fix routed 1D gate biases to AdamW (Muon expects 2D). Commit e559446.

By 2026-05-02 the architecture was in place, the MQAR probe was the standing gate, and the Wo init story was a real architectural finding rather than just a debug save. Stage 2's MQAR result was mixed — it saturated 25-50 steps slower than baseline at d4/T=128 with high seed variance — but the architecture worked: gates alive from step 1, prefix-log-product math correct. The next move was a real d6 pretrain.

03 / The d6 / d8 verdict

Neutral on val_bpb, twice over

The "+1.8% SFT win" headline

Stage 2's d6 pretrain landed at val_bpb 1.1743 (parity with the baseline's 1.174). SFT on top of it landed at 0.6518, beating the historical baseline's 0.6639 by 1.8%. That looked like real architectural signal. A2 (three SFT seeds on the same Stage 2 pretrain → spread 0.0004) ruled out an SFT-seed lottery.

A3-prime overturned the headline

The decisive move was A3-prime: re-run a modern-recipe vanilla d6 baseline (matching all the small recipe drift between historical baseline and current master — most prominently the ve_gate Muon→AdamW change in 840d3db). Modern-recipe baseline pretrain: 1.1686. SFT: 0.6483. Beats both Stage 2 seeds. The "+1.8% SFT win" was overwhelmingly recipe drift, not architecture. Documented in full at docs/hope_nl_a3_a3prime_2026-05-05.md.

The d8 cross-check

A separate "does depth pull memory ahead" cross-check came via the d8 overnight pretrain (2026-05-10, val_bpb 1.076 at 5000 iter / 82M tokens) and d8 SFT-A2 at matched token budget (2026-05-11, val_bpb 0.6218, ChatCORE 0.1622). d8 essentially ties d6_stage2 on ChatCORE (0.1622 vs 0.1744 within stderr at -x 100). The d8 result did not change the architectural picture for memory — bigger plain attention is just as good as memory-bearing d6 at this scale.

Stage 4 deferred

Stage 4 (multi-block memory, the architecture's next layer of expressiveness) was conditional on Stage 2 surviving. Condition not met. Deferred per ADR-008. The d6/d8 from-scratch arc concluded with Stage 2 sitting at neutral on what LM val_bpb measures.

The methodological lesson: recipe drift is real, large, and silent. Roughly 0.016 of "free" SFT improvement had accumulated across 4 commits between the historical baseline checkpoint and current master — dwarfing the architectural delta. Every future architecture-class experiment needs a same-day re-baseline, not a comparison against an archived number.

04 / The strategic pivot

ADR-008 · pause the LM arc, sharpen the question

On 2026-05-13 the project formally paused the d6/d8 from-scratch arc. The strategic pivot doc (docs/strategic_pivot_2026-05-13.md) articulates two related decisions:

  1. Future memory-mechanism research moves to a small synthetic harness. Depth 2-4, MQAR + selective copy + induction heads, cycle < 5 min/variant. The d6/5000-iter LM budget (~3-18 h) is too slow for architectural iteration when the signal-to-noise per minute at probe scale is so much better.
  2. "Own every parameter" only earns its pretrain cost when the change requires from-scratch retraining. For LoRA-class additions to a frozen base, the right base isn't a d6/74M nanochat — it's a 4-8B local model running through MLX. This is what the bonsai cross-arch evaluation thread was setting up.

Crucially, "the graft target idea" — slot a memory mechanism onto a frozen 4-8B base — was not the chosen path. The operator's interest in bonsai was downstream (feedback loops, distillation, judge-models), not as somewhere to inject new memory layers.

The pivot wasn't surrender; it was a sharper question. The d6/d8 LM-loss aggregate had stopped distinguishing hypotheses. The right next move was a tool that could distinguish them.

05 / Side threads

What got built around the spine

Five threads ran alongside the architectural arc. Some answered different questions (chat quality, persona retention). Some built tooling that the spine needed (preflight, grad checkpointing, eval-loop hardening). One — the δ-mem port — produced the reframing that the spine then converged on.

δ-mem MLX port
May 17-18 · feeds the spine

Ported Lei et al.'s δ-mem to MLX, evaluated on Qwen3-4B-8bit. Lifts 3/3 multi-step generation tasks (HumanEval +12.2pp, MBPP +8pp, GSM8K +5.5pp); flat on 4/4 knowledge tasks; null on the paper's own long-context recall benchmark (HotpotQA).

Verdict: working-memory adapter, not long-term memory. Same axis-of-value bench v0 found 2 days later at 100× smaller scale.

Bonsai cross-arch eval
May 15-17 · tool, sweet-spot found

Built chat_eval_mlx.py (six-task ChatCORE for any MLX-loadable model) + lenient extractor (handles non-nanochat output formats). Ran Bonsai-1.7B/4B/8B-int2 and fp-Qwen3-4B-8bit at -x 200.

Verdict: 4B-int2 ChatCORE 0.6456, 8B-int2 0.7056, fp-Qwen3-4B-8bit 0.7656. Precision beats parameter count. 4B is the operational pick for local chat / distillation source / judge.

Training-perf arc
May 9-10 · enabling infrastructure

--grad-checkpoint (Block.forward checkpointed) + --no-compile. Two patches in one: ~12% wall-clock win in pressured-but-feasible regimes (compile on), capacity unlock for d12/batch=8 (compile off). Bug fix: torch.compile's inductor scheduler masks the patch's memory savings.

Verdict: d8 overnight pretrain on M2 24GB became feasible at single-grad-accum. Enabled the d8 capacity verdict in §03.

LoRA arc (L1 persona)
May 7 · adapter ceiling at 74M

L1 v2 (rank-16, Q+K+V+O target) solved persona retention: 0/30 → 19/30 all_three on held-out personas. Template-bleed appeared ("You're [X]" opener fires on every prompt). v3 (dataset diversity) regressed three-axis to 3/30. Working theory: rank-16 LoRA at 74M base is a one-pattern adapter.

Verdict: dataset/training-time levers at d6/74M have hit a ceiling. The strict-sense capacity argument now points at bigger base + LoRA, which the bonsai thread set up.

Quant arc (STE binary/ternary)
May 7-8 · clarifying ADR

Ported BinaryLinear/TernaryLinear from trx4mr. STE training works mechanically at d3 (validates infra) but pays +33-46% wall and +6.8-9.9% val_bpb gap. ADR-007 clarified the memory accounting: STE training does not save memory (latent fp32 + STE op). The 14× memory win lives at inference via pack-to-1-bit.

Verdict: paused as a research direction on M2. The bonsai thread captures the same prize cheaper.

Chat-quality thread
May 6 · diagnostic-only

Operator-driven reframe: "d6 chatbot is kinda bad." Tested SFT-rebalance (cut MMLU/GSM8K → chatmix_a) and SFT-additive (2× SmolTalk). Both moved val_bpb by 0.05+ (huge by this codebase's standards) but the 7-prompt multi-turn rubric went 0/7 in either direction — differently broken, not less broken. Cosine-NN diagnostic (from sibling trx4mr Phase 5 retro): ~65% right-context drift, ~18% left-context collision.

Verdict: at d6/74M, recipe-rebalancing is rearranging which templates dominate. Real fix lives in capacity (bigger base) or codebook (binary/ternary, deferred per ADR-007).

The pattern across the side-threads: tools that endured (chat_eval_mlx, grad-checkpoint, preflight, inherit-from, MPS fragmentation infrastructure) became part of the workbench. Adapter-class experiments hit a ceiling at d6/74M — the rank-16 LoRA result and the SFT-rebalance result point at the same thing from opposite directions. The δ-mem detour did something none of the others did: it generated a finding that didn't fit the spine's "neutral" verdict, and that mismatch was the prompt for bench v0.

06 / The reframe

δ-mem pointed at the right axis

On 2026-05-17 the δ-mem paper (Lei et al., May 2026) landed in scope. The reference implementation was CUDA-only for training but the published rank-8 adapter (Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 backbone) was inference-viable on M2. We ported the forward math to MLX (nanochat/delta_mem_mlx.py, ~230 LOC, math verified to 2e-6 against the torch reference) and ran it through our own chat_eval_mlx.py harness extended with a --delta-mem flag.

The result didn't match the paper's framing of δ-mem as "long-term memory." Per-task pattern across 388 minutes of eval wall:

Task classTasksΔ vs Qwen3-4B-8bit baseline
Multi-step generationHumanEval, GSM8K, MBPP3/3 lift · combined p < 0.001
Knowledge / categoricalARC-E, ARC-C, MMLU, SpellingBee4/4 flat · all |z| < 0.7
Long-context recallHotpotQA (paper's own headline)null · ΔF1 = +0.0003 over n=200

Three out of three multi-step generation tasks lifted significantly. Four out of four knowledge tasks were flat. The paper's headline benchmark (HotpotQA, long-context recall) was null. The axis isn't long-term memory — it's selective state-tracking during generation. Whatever else δ-mem is, on this setup it is a working-memory adapter that helps tasks where the model has to write content, ignore noise, and carry selective state forward over many output tokens.

Full writeup at docs/delta_mem_field_result_2026-05-18.html. The clean methodological observation: the paper's framing of its own mechanism was wrong on the benchmark it sells with. The mechanism is real, the axis-of-value is different from the published claim.

That observation had immediate implications for the paused Hope/NL track. The original Stage 1.5 probe (MQAR) tests recall; it cannot distinguish "memory engaged" from "memory engaged on the right axis." If δ-mem's axis was real, Stage 2's "neutral on val_bpb" might be an aggregate of two opposite effects: wins on selective state, losses on uniform recall, canceling out on a mixed corpus. That hypothesis was directly testable in the synthetic harness ADR-008 had already prescribed. The reframe was what bench v0 needed to know which probes to build.

07 / The convergence

bench v0 — Stage 2's task signature, surfaced

From 2026-05-18 to 2026-05-20, the bench/ playground got built and run. ~300 LOC of new code (bench/tasks.py, bench/run.py, marker), two probes, three architectural arms, eleven runs, ~250 min of M2 compute. Full writeup at docs/bench_v0_result_2026-05-20.html. The findings:

Stage 1 vs Stage 2 specialize on opposite axes

Architecture SelectiveCopy (state-tracking) MQAR hard (uniform recall)
baseline (attn + MLP) sat 200 sat 225
Stage 1 (fixed-gate linear memory) sat 375 (1.88× slower) sat 250 (≈ baseline)
Stage 2 (learned per-token α/η) sat 100 (2× faster than baseline) sat 375 (1.67× slower)

Stage 2 has a clean signature: 2× faster than baseline on selective state-tracking, 1.67× slower on uniform recall. Stage 1 has the mirror image: ≈ baseline on recall, severely worse on state-tracking. The two memory architectures occupy different functional niches. Plain attention is the universal middle.

η_init can't fix MQAR — the cost is architectural

Five-arm sweep of η_init prior at {0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 0.8, 0.99} on Stage 2 hard MQAR produced a non-monotonic U-curve. Best: η=0.99 at sat 325. Worst: η=0.5 at sat 475. Baseline: 225. Even at the best prior, Stage 2 trails baseline by 100 steps. Tuning the prior covers ~50 steps of the gap; the remaining ~75-100 steps are an irreducible architectural floor — the cost of having learnable gate parameters in the optimization budget plus the inductive bias of "selectivity is possible." Stage 2 doesn't just need to learn the task; it needs to learn not to use its own selectivity.

The cross-scale convergence

Mechanism Selective state-tracking Uniform recall
δ-mem · Qwen3-4B-8bit · 4B params HumanEval +12.2pp, MBPP +8pp, GSM8K +5.5pp HotpotQA ΔF1 = +0.0003
Stage 2 · d4 nanochat · 37M params SC sat 100 vs baseline 200 MQAR sat 375 vs baseline 225

Two completely different architectural mechanisms (rank-8 recurrent state with delta-rule updates vs additive linear-attention with per-token learned α/η), at scales 100× apart, on different tasks (real-world coding/math vs synthetic state-tracking and recall), show the same axis-of-value. What they share is learned selectivity at the per-token level. Both have parameters that decide, conditional on the input, how much to write into and read out of memory state. Fixed-gate architectures (Stage 1) don't have this knob. Pure-attention architectures (baseline) don't have memory state at all.

The d6 "neutral" verdict gained a mechanism

The 2026-05-05 synthesis concluded: Stage 2 is neutral, not net-positive, on natural-language val_bpb at d6/5000-iter on ClimbMix. That conclusion stands. bench v0 doesn't overturn it — it explains it. Natural language is a mix of selective and uniform demands. Stage 2's wins and losses on the two axes cancel out on aggregate val_bpb. The aggregate was correct; the underlying architecture has more to say. The d6/d8 LM arc cost ~30 h of M2 wall to conclude "neutral, defer." bench v0 cost ~2.5 h to add "wins on selective, loses on uniform, prior tuning is non-monotonic, architectural floor is irreducible." Different questions, different tools.

08 / What we're looking for now

The frontier · three concrete questions

The originating question has split. Two sub-questions, both partly answered:

  • "Does Hope/NL help LM val_bpb at our scale?" Answered. No. d6/d8 on ClimbMix is neutral under recipe-controlled comparison.
  • "Does the architecture help on the tasks where memory should matter?" Partly answered. Yes at d4 on synthetic state-tracking (Stage 2 SC 2× win). Crossed-validated cross-mechanism by δ-mem at 100× scale. Not yet confirmed at the architecture's native scale (d6+), not yet bounded by seed variance, not yet generalized to a third probe.

The interesting structural question is no longer "is memory good?" but "are memory mechanisms architecturally specialized, and what's the routing rule?" Live concrete priorities:

  1. n=2-3 seeds on the four bench v0 headline numbers.~2 h wall · cheapest, most important
    Stage 2 SC sat=100 (the win), Stage 2 MQAR sat=375 (the loss), η=0.5 sat=475 (U-curve worst), Stage 1 SC sat=375 (the mirror loss). Every current claim above this line is "n=1 seed." Bound the seed-noise component before any further architectural claim.
  2. η_init sweep on hard SC (the mirror experiment).~100 min wall
    The MQAR U-curve says no global prior is optimal on recall. Is SC robustly good across η, or does it have its own U-shape with η=0.1 happening to land on the lucky side? This determines whether the bench v0 "Stage 2 wins SC" finding is structural or prior-dependent.
  3. Stage 2 at d6 on hard MQAR + SC.~200 min wall · bridges back to the LM arc
    Does the d4 specialization survive at d6? If yes, the original LM "neutral" verdict gets its mechanism at the architecture's native scale, and the prediction "Stage 2 should win on selective-state-heavy benchmarks at d6" becomes testable.

Lower-priority but live:

  • Induction-heads probe as a third axis between recall and state-tracking. The δ-mem axis and the SC vs MQAR axis are similar but not identical. A third probe would either consolidate the two-axis story or split it.
  • Input-conditional gate priors. A small MLP on global input statistics that picks η_init per input. Tests whether the task-mix problem dissolves when priors are learnable rather than fixed — i.e., whether the architectural floor we found is intrinsic or just a poorly-chosen single-knob.
  • Architectural variants on the niche. Swap topology vs additive, different memory layer placements, different gate parameterizations. Cheap on bench (each variant ~20 min), informative only if the seed-stability and d6-reproduction priorities have already cleared.

The 2026-05-13 strategic-pivot decision still holds: the d6/d8 from-scratch LM arc is paused; bench is where memory-mechanism iteration happens. The pivot bought us a ~10× cheaper iteration loop, and bench v0 used the first ~2.5 hours of that loop to produce more architectural traction than the prior 30+ hours of LM-loss work had.

09 / What we're not doing

The closed paths

These were live questions at some point. They aren't anymore, and the doc-level rationale matters for not re-opening them by accident:

  • Bigger from-scratch pretrains (d12+, 5B+ tokens). M2 wall budget doesn't permit. Per CLAUDE.md and the d8 capacity findings, d12 canonical Chinchilla is ~230 hours on this hardware. Not a live path until compute changes.
  • Stage 4 (multi-block memory). Deferred per ADR-008. Conditional on Stage 2 surviving at d6; the condition is still not met. Bench v0 sharpens but does not change that calculus.
  • The Hope-Attention variant (Stage 5). A real design block (~1-1.5 weeks). Not justified until Stage 2's d6 result is positive on the axis bench v0 cares about.
  • Bigger LoRA variants on d6 nanochat. v3 falsified the dataset-diversity hypothesis; the rank-16 ceiling is an artifact of base capacity, not of the LoRA itself. Path forward for "useful local chatbot" runs through Bonsai-4B or Qwen3-4B-8bit + adapter, not d6 + adapter.
  • STE binary/ternary training on M2. ADR-007 closes the memory-accounting case: STE training does not save memory; the 14× win lives at inference via pack-to-1-bit. Bonsai already paid that cost on bigger compute; we use it as a tool rather than rebuilding it.
  • Cross-arch chatbot quality comparisons. Bonsai-4B beats d6_stage2 by 3.70× on ChatCORE, fp-Qwen3-4B-8bit by 4.39×. The cross-arch question is settled at this hardware envelope.
10 / Coda

What the project actually is now

The originating question was specific (does Hope/NL help LM val_bpb?) and the d6/d8 answer is specific (no, neutral, under recipe-controlled comparison). That answer is now load-bearing. It's also not the most interesting thing the fork has produced.

The more durable finding is the architectural shape: learned per-token selectivity is a specialization, not a universal improvement. It buys you state-tracking. It costs you uniform recall. Plain attention is the median tool that handles both tolerably; memory mechanisms are sharp tools that handle one well and the other badly. Two independent mechanisms at scales 100× apart converged on the same pattern, which makes it harder to dismiss as a single-architecture quirk.

The project's working hypothesis going into the next move is therefore: memory mechanisms are architecturally additive only on tasks they're matched to. The design game is task-mechanism routing, not "memory yes/no." Whether that hypothesis is true at d6 on natural-language task subsets, whether it survives seed variance, and whether a third axis (induction-heads) reorganizes the picture again — those are the next experiments. They're all bench-cheap. The first one (n=2-3 seeds) is the most important and the least exciting.

For anyone returning to this fork after time away, the spine to keep in mind is: originating question → d6 verdict (neutral) → side-thread that didn't fit (δ-mem) → reframe (selective axis) → convergence (bench v0) → frontier (robustness, scale, third axis). The bench is the live work surface. The d6/d8 LM arc is reference material. Side-threads either fed the spine (δ-mem), built the workbench (perf, eval, preflight), or hit ceilings worth knowing about (LoRA, STE quant, SFT rebalancing). The map ends where the seed-robustness experiment begins.
