# L1 v3 — diverse training mix (FALSIFIED)

**Date:** 2026-05-07 (later session, same day as v1/v2)
**Verdict:** v3 hypothesis falsified. Dataset diversity (80/20 persona +
chit-chat with T6-phrasing-diverse persona re-curation) at this size/ratio
made the LoRA *worse* on every metric. v2 stays the best L1-d6 fp adapter
to date.

---

## Hypothesis under test

The v2 LoRA (HANDOFF.md Day 2026-05-07 + Postscript) solved persona
retention (0/30 → 19/30 all_three on held-out) but exhibited
**template-bleed**: the "You're [Name], a [Role] in [Location]…"
opener stamped onto unrelated prompts. The handoff plan for v3:

> ~80 % persona-retention + ~20 % SmolTalk-flavoured chit-chat where
> the right answer is *not* "You're [X]"

The expectation: chit-chat dilution would teach the LoRA "not every
prompt should be answered with persona-recall shape" without sacrificing
the persona-recall win. The post-hoc refinement (this session): also
re-curate the 300 persona rows with explicit T6-phrasing diversity —
across a batch of 10, no two T6s start with the same 3 words — to test
whether template-bleed was driven by the *single specific phrasing*
("You're [Name]…") rather than the *single shape* (recall-form).

The two refinements stack as a single v3 dataset:

| component               | rows | source                                     |
|-------------------------|-----:|--------------------------------------------|
| persona, T6-diverse      | 300  | `persona_retention_v1_diverse.jsonl` (new) |
| chit-chat                |  75  | `chit_chat_v1.jsonl` (new)                 |
| **total v3 train mix**   | 375  | `lora_v3_train.jsonl` (shuffled, seed=42)  |

The held-out 30-persona eval set (`persona_retention_v1_eval.jsonl`)
was NOT re-curated — kept identical so v3 numbers are directly
comparable to v2's.

## Curation

Two curators, both Claude CLI headless via the operator's subscription
(no `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`). Reused the pulse-style pattern from v2.

**`dev/curate_persona_retention.py`** got two changes:
- `SYSTEM_PROMPT` extended with explicit T6-opener-diversity guidance
  (8 sample shapes; "no two T6 replies start with the same 3 words").
- `--tag` arg parameterising the output filename so v1 stayed intact.

Run: `--n-train 300 --n-eval 0 --tag v1_diverse`.

**`dev/curate_chit_chat.py`** is new (~280 LOC). Sibling structure: same
JSON-schema-enforced batch shape (6 turns alternating user/assistant),
different `SYSTEM_PROMPT` requiring T1 *not* to introduce a persona, T5
*not* to be a recall question, T6 *not* to begin with `"You're"`,
`"You are"`, `"Your name"`, or `"You mentioned"`. Validator enforces
those banned-pattern checks plus the role pattern; no substring check.

Curation outcomes:

| dataset                | rows | rejects | cost  | notable diversity                         |
|------------------------|-----:|--------:|------:|-------------------------------------------|
| persona-diverse v1     |  300 |     21  | $2.62 | 128 names / 118 roles / 111 locations; **10.3 %** of T6s start with `"You're"` (vs ~all of v1's pre-eval persona-recall outputs); top openers: `right — you`, `sure — your`, `sorry, almost forgot`, `that's right —`, `if i remember` |
| chit-chat v1           |   75 |      0  | $0.48 | 72 unique topics; 75/75 unique 3-word T6 openers; **0 %** banned-pattern openers (validator-enforced) |
| **total**              |  375 |     21  | **$3.10** |                                       |

Curation took ~20 min wall in parallel.

## Training

`scripts/chat_sft_lora` with v2 hyperparams held constant — only
`--data-path` and `--lora-tag` changed:

```
--rank 16 --alpha 32 --target c_q,c_k,c_v,c_proj
--num-iterations 600 --lr 3e-4 --device-batch-size 4
--val-fraction 0.05 --warmup-ratio 0.05 --warmdown-ratio 0.5 --seed 42
```

Wall: **4.0 min** on M2 (vs v2's 1.95 min — 25 % more rows + slight
data-loader overhead). Adapter: **1.14 MB** (same shape as v2, expected).
val_bpb=1.18 at step 599 (vs v2's 0.82) — much higher because val now
mixes persona AND chit-chat instead of pure recall, and chit-chat is
intrinsically harder to fit at this scale.

## Evaluation

Three signals, same harnesses as v2.

### Held-out 30-persona eval — **regression**

| metric          | base (n=30)     | v2 (rank 16 Q+K+V+O) | **v3** (diverse mix)  |
|-----------------|----------------:|---------------------:|----------------------:|
| name_recall     |  6.7 % (2/30)   | 80.0 % (24/30)       | **46.7 % (14/30)**    |
| role_recall     | 33.3 % (10/30)  | 86.7 % (26/30)       | **60.0 % (18/30)**    |
| location_recall | 13.3 % (4/30)   | 83.3 % (25/30)       | **46.7 % (14/30)**    |
| **all_three**   | **0.0 % (0/30)**| **63.3 % (19/30)**   | **10.0 % (3/30)**     |

v3 lost **16/30** of v2's all_three wins. Not a graceful
graceful-degradation curve — many outputs are now *malformed* rather
than just incomplete:

- Token-loop degeneration (row 5: `"You're James O'Brien, Dublin, Dublin, Dublin, Dublin, Dublin…"`)
- Confused phrasings (row 16: `"Yes! Keiko studied sustainable gardens."` — drops role and location entirely)
- Truncated/fragment replies (row 13: `"Janne Virtanen, a pastry chef — yes, I love working with."`)
- The Moscow → Houston substitution observed in v2 **persists** at v3 (rows 9, 22).

The diverse openers *do* fire across many rows (`Right — you mentioned…`,
`Sure — your name's…`, `Sorry, almost…`, etc.), so the curator did
shift the distribution of T6 phrasings. But the model didn't learn any
single phrasing well enough to be reliable — capacity got spread
across many shapes, none firmly grounded.

### 7-prompt rubric (`dev/multi_turn_eval`) — **regression**

| prompt                 | base | v2   | **v3** | result                                                        |
|------------------------|------|------|--------|---------------------------------------------------------------|
| persona_retention      | fail | PASS | PASS*  | v3 PASSes on substring presence but phrasing is broken: T4 = `"Sorry, almost every name — your name, you're a software engineer named Alex, and you're a software engineer at a startup."` |
| reference_resolution   | fail | fail | fail   | v3: `"I remember you're Mittens — both are you with a cat…"` (still treats user as cat) |
| numerical_thread       | fail | fail | fail   | v3 hallucinates 248 apples / nonsense math                    |
| topic_stickiness       | fail | fail | fail   | v3: `"Mia's a huge city to have!"` — drops Portland entirely  |
| constraint_accumulation| fail | fail | fail   | v3: `"A vacation with a vacation is an amazing way…"` — fragments |
| self_correction        | fail | **PASS** | **fail** | v2 cleanly said "Canberra is the capital, not Sydney"; v3 says `"In the capital of Australia, Sydney has a city's population."` — restates Sydney as capital |
| open_drift             | fail | fail | fail   | v3 fragments across all turns                                 |

**Aggregate: base 0/7 → v2 2/7 → v3 1/7.** v3 lost the
self_correction win v2 had earned incidentally.

The "You're [Name], a [Role] in [Location]" stamp shape is mostly absent
from v3 outputs. But mid-sentence "you're" still fires routinely, and
the *content* is broken across the board — token loops, fragmented
sentences, irrelevant replies. **The bleed didn't disappear; it morphed
into general output degradation.**

### Free-chat smoke (`dev/free_chat_smoke`) — **bad in a new way**

Postscript-grade 4-turn transcript at scale=1.0:

| turn | user                            | v3 assistant                                                                                              |
|------|---------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 1    | hi there                        | `Nice, the same thing, has an important space for the project. The fundamental space is a lot of important things.` |
| 2    | What's the capital of France?   | `Lea, the capital of France — yes, but it's a bit more innovative and practical.`                          |
| 3    | Why is the sky blue?            | `And how much you'd like to see the sky blue?`                                                            |
| 4    | Who are you?                    | `Well, yes, the sky is the sky blue. You're really really blue, and you're really into a dream of creating a sky with a space.` |

The narrow regex bleed-check (start-of-line `"You're "` etc.) PASSES
for v3 — but the outputs are still meaningfully bleed-flavoured and
wholly unhelpful (T2 invents a name "Lea"; T4 contains
"You're really really blue"). The smoke regex was rubric-narrow; it
caught what we trained the curator to remove (the start-of-line stamp)
but not the underlying degradation.

## Falsification verdict

| signal          | v2 vs base          | v3 vs v2                | v3 vs base                |
|-----------------|---------------------|-------------------------|---------------------------|
| persona all_three | +63.3 pp          | **−53.3 pp regression** | +10.0 pp (still positive)|
| rubric pass     | 0/7 → 2/7           | 2/7 → 1/7 regression    | 0/7 → 1/7                |
| free-chat usability | bleed-stamped, unusable | shape changed, still unusable | comparable           |

**Falsified hypotheses:**
- *Dataset shape diversity (chit-chat dilution) at 80/20 ratio fixes
  template-bleed.* No — bleed shape changed but underlying degradation
  remains; recall crashed.
- *T6-phrasing diversity in persona data fixes template-bleed.*
  Inconclusive in isolation — v3 stacked both interventions, can't
  attribute to one.

**Working theory of failure** (not falsified, narrows the search):
- 600 iters held constant means each persona row got ~25 % fewer
  passes than v2 (375 vs 300 rows in mix). v3 may simply be
  undertrained on the persona slice.
- Diverse-T6 prompt taught the model many shapes weakly instead of one
  shape strongly. Capacity at rank 16 / 74M base may be insufficient
  to learn multiple recall phrasings.
- 75 chit-chat rows is too few to seed competent free-chat — they
  just diluted the recall signal without teaching anything useful.

## Cleanest single observation

v2's strength was its *narrowness*: one shape, learned hard, fires
everywhere. v3 broke v2 along all three eval axes by trying to broaden
the shape distribution at constant capacity. A LoRA at rank 16 / 74M
base appears to be a one-pattern adapter; trying to teach it many
patterns at once at fixed compute spreads the capacity into ineffective
mush.

## Where this leaves the project

The L1-d6 fp arc verdict from end of session:

- **v2 remains the best L1-d6 fp adapter.** 19/30 all_three on held-out,
  2/7 on rubric, characterised template-bleed. To use it: `--lora-scale 1.0`
  for persona-recall demos, `--lora-scale 0.0` (or no `--lora-tag`) for
  free chat. No good middle ground (postscript: scale=0.5 also bleeds).
- **v3 is shelved.** The dataset/training-time levers tested here didn't
  help.

Three plausible follow-ups, in cost order:

1. **Train v3 longer.** Same 80/20 dataset, same hyperparams,
   `--num-iterations 1200`. Tests the under-training hypothesis. ~8 min
   wall, $0 (data is curated).
2. **v3-narrow: just diverse-T6 persona, no chit-chat.** Isolates the
   T6-phrasing-diversity intervention. Same dataset minus chit-chat
   = 300 rows. ~3 min wall, $0.
3. **v3-loose: just chit-chat dilution, original v1 persona.** Isolates
   the shape-dilution intervention. ~3 min wall, $0.

If all three fail, dataset/training-time interventions at d6/74M may
have hit a ceiling. The architectural arms (LiGO / bert2BERT depth
growth, both newly logged in `docs/project_notes/backlog.md`; or the
preserved Bonsai-LoRA Phase 2 priority) become the next candidates.
