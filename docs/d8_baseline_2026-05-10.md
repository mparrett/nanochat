## Run config

```
scripts/base_train.py --depth=8 --num-iterations=5000 \
  --device-batch-size=8 --total-batch-size=16384 \
  --grad-checkpoint \
  --eval-every=1000 --eval-tokens=5242880 \
  --core-metric-every=-1 --sample-every=-1 \
  --save-every=1000 --save-keep-last-n=2 \
  --model-tag=d8_overnight
```

- **82 M tokens trained** (5000 × 16 384). Sub-Chinchilla — d8's
  Chinchilla budget at ratio 12 is ~1.5 B tokens, so this is ~5 % of
  canonical.
- **Single-grad-accum** (total-batch = device-batch). Effective
  per-step batch is **32× smaller than d6 canonical** (524 K → 16 K
  tokens). Gradient noise per step is consequently higher; pretrains
  with much larger effective batch typically converge to a different
  trajectory.
- `--grad-checkpoint` on. `--no-compile` off (compile enabled).
  Chosen after the d8 capacity probe found batch=12 paging-strangled,
  batch=16 OOM, so batch=8 was the working ceiling.
- Started 02:54:31 PDT, finished 21:00:51 PDT. **Wall clock 18 h 6 m**
  for 5000 iters.

### Variance caveat

`--max-per-task=100` means each task's accuracy is estimated from 100
samples. Stderr on the per-task centered values is **~5 % (multiple
choice) to ~10 % (language modeling)**, depending on task. The mean
across 22 tasks averages down, but individual task numbers (especially
the headline outliers like +0.37 for bigbench_cs_algorithms) shouldn't
be treated as precise — directional only. Canonical `--max-per-task=500`
would tighten by ~√5 = 2.24×, at ~5× the wall.

## What this run answered (and didn't)

**Answered:**
- d8 trains stably on M2 at single-grad-accum / batch=8 / compile + gc.
  No NaNs, no loss spikes, smooth descent through 5000 iters.
- val_bpb beats d6 baseline by 8 % at matched iter count — depth helps
  even at much smaller effective batch.
- CORE shows the model is meaningfully above random on most ICL tasks;
  strong on bigbench_cs_algorithms (+0.37) and arc_easy (+0.25).

**Not answered:**
- **Does d8 beat d6 at matched compute or matched tokens?** Today's
  comparison is matched-iter, which heavily favors d8 since each iter
  is much cheaper than d6 canonical. A fair comparison would need
  either (a) d6 truncated to 82 M tokens, or (b) d8 trained to a
  larger token budget approaching Chinchilla.
- **Does d8 at full Chinchilla scale (~1.5 B tokens) close the gap to
  GPT-2 (CORE 0.257)?** Today's 0.081 doesn't say. d6 at canonical
  2.6 B tokens lands at its own CORE (need to look up); the extrapolation
  to d8 at 1.5 B is speculation.
- **Canonical CORE (`--max-per-task=500`)** wasn't run. Today's 0.081
  is the quick-mode number with real variance per task. If this number
  is going into a leaderboard or formal comparison, re-run at canonical
  before citing.

## Operational lessons (referenced into key_facts.md)

Three lessons surfaced today, codified in the same conversation:

1. **Brief benchmarks under-predict long-run pace on a noisy M2.**
   Pre-launch 6 s/step bench → 10-25 s/step actual long-run average.
   Solution: run benches AFTER background-process cleanup. Encoded
   in `dev/preflight_memory.py` + key_facts §"Pre-launch hygiene".
2. **Wandb-init failure was a real defect, not just my paranoia.**
   `wandb.init()` was unwrapped in all 4 training scripts; failure
   would propagate and kill the run. Fixed (try/except → DummyWandb
   fallback) so wandb can now be defaulted on without risk.
   Encoded in key_facts §"Logging — wandb preferred for any
   non-trivial run".
3. **Background workloads dominate paging cost on M2 24 GB.** Two
   cleanups during the run cut step-time by 2-4×. ~5 min of cleanup
   is consistently worth it for long runs.
