Two weeks ago the Hope/NL track concluded with Stage 2 is neutral on LM val_bpb under recipe-controlled comparison at d6/5000-iter and got paused. A δ-mem detour last weekend produced a finding that didn't fit that verdict: working-memory mechanisms lift multi-step generation tasks but don't touch long-context recall. To test whether the same axis shows up in our own architecture, we built a fast-iteration probe harness — bench v0, eight runs in 2½ hours of compute — and watched Stage 2 saturate 2× faster than baseline on SelectiveCopy while losing by 1.67× on MQAR. Three more runs swept the η_init prior; none closed the gap. The architecture has wins and losses on opposite task axes, and the d6/5000-iter LM aggregate averaged them away.

"Stage 2 is neutral, not net-positive, on natural-language val_bpb." — Hope/NL track synthesis, 2026-05-05

The synthesis verdict from two weeks ago stands on what it measured. The bench v0 result doesn't overturn it; it adds internal structure. Stage 2 has wins (selective state tracking) and losses (uniform recall). Natural language is a mix of both demands, so the two effects net out close to zero on val_bpb. The aggregate was correct; the underlying architecture has more to say.

01 / Context

Where the track was paused, and why we picked it up again

The Hope/NL arc finished its from-scratch d6/d8 pretrain phase on 2026-05-13 with ADR-008: pause the LM-loss arc, move memory-mechanism research into a much smaller iteration loop (depth 2–4, synthetic diagnostic tasks, <5 min per architectural variant). The d6 result was clean: Stage 2 (learned-α/η additive memory at block 1) is neutral on val_bpb at recipe parity — the original "+1.8% SFT win" was overwhelmingly recipe drift, and once recipe was controlled, the architectural delta sat inside seed noise.

"Neutral, not bad" was the right call. The architecture wasn't broken — the MQAR probe with the W_o init fix showed Stage 1 memory matching baseline on synthetic recall. But the LM-loss aggregate gave us no traction on what the architecture was actually doing differently. That's the gap the bench v0 playground was built to fill.

The δ-mem detour pointed at the right diagnostic axis

The weekend before this work, we ported δ-mem (Lei et al., May 2026) to MLX and ran it against our six-task ChatCORE suite plus MBPP and HotpotQA. The full writeup is at delta_mem_field_result_2026-05-18.html. The clean empirical finding:

  • Lifts on three multi-step generation tasks: HumanEval +12.2pp (2.33σ), GSM8K +5.5pp, MBPP +8.0pp (Fisher's p < 0.001 combined).
  • Flat on four knowledge tasks: ARC-Easy, ARC-Challenge, MMLU, SpellingBee (all |z| < 0.7).
  • Flat on the paper's own headline long-context recall benchmark: HotpotQA Δ mean F1 = +0.0003 across 200 examples.

The implication: δ-mem isn't a long-term-memory mechanism (the paper's framing) but a working-memory one. The axis that lifts is multi-step generation requiring selective state across many output tokens, not retrieval across long contexts. Whatever δ-mem is doing structurally, it's the kind of thing that helps when you need to write content, ignore noise, and carry selective state forward.

That observation needed to be tested against our own architecture. If δ-mem's working-memory signature is real, Hope/NL's Stage 2 (learned per-token α/η gates on linear-attention memory) might do the same thing for the same reason. The original Stage 1.5 probe (MQAR) only tests recall; it can't distinguish "memory engaged" from "memory engaged on the right axis." We needed a state-tracking probe.

02 / The bench harness

Two probes, three architectural arms, <25 min per variant

bench/tasks.py defines a small Task ABC plus two diagnostic probes; bench/run.py is a single-file CLI runner that threads `--task` and the existing `--hope-*` flags into a small nanochat GPT with random init. JSONL logs to bench/logs/<label>.jsonl, one record per eval step.

Two probes, oriented to the two memory axes we care about:

  • MQAR (Multi-Query Associative Recall — ported from dev/probe_mqar.py). Recall axis: sequence is bos, k₁,v₁, …, k_K,v_K, sep, q₁,a₁, …, q_M,a_M; model must look up the value bound to each query key. Tests pure key→value recall.
  • SelectiveCopy (Mamba's canonical state-tracking probe — new in this bench). State-tracking axis: K content tokens scattered through a length-T_in noise stream; after a separator, model must copy the content in order. Tests selective gating: write content, ignore noise.

Three architectural arms across both probes:

  • baseline — vanilla nanochat block (attention + MLP at every layer). No memory module.
  • Stage 1 — additive linear-attention memory at block 1, fixed α = η = 1 (no gating), W_o init = 1.0 (the Stage 1.5b fix).
  • Stage 2 — same topology, learned per-token α/η via α_t = α_max · σ(W_α x_t + b_α) and η_t = η_max · σ(W_η x_t + b_η). Canonical priors: α_init = 0.99, η_init = 0.1.

All runs at d4 (n_embd = 256, ~37M params), seed = 0, eval-every 25 steps, device-batch 64. Wall budget: 16–25 min per variant on MPS.

The mechanism

Stage 2's learned per-token α and η give the model the ability to selectively write to and read from memory. Stage 1's α = η = 1 commits the model to writing every token into memory and retaining it forever. These are different inductive biases:

  • Uniform-write tasks (MQAR): every position is meaningful, and the optimal policy is "write everything, never forget." Stage 1's fixed α = η = 1 is exactly this policy at init. Stage 2 has to learn to push its gates near 1 across all positions — the η_init = 0.1 prior puts it far from optimum.
  • Selective-write tasks (SC): some positions (content) should be written, others (noise) ignored. Stage 1's "write everything" injects noise into memory, which the rest of the model has to suppress. Stage 2's η_init = 0.1 happens to start near the right policy ("write small amounts by default"); the model just learns to push η up at content positions.

The bench result is now this: 

             | uniform-write    | selective-write
-------------|------------------|----------------
 Stage 1     | small overhead   | severe penalty
 (fixed α=η=1)| (always write)   | (writes noise too)
-------------|------------------|----------------
 Stage 2     | severe penalty   | net win
 (learned α/η)| (gate optimization
              |  budget + bias)  | (selectivity is the right tool)

Stage 1 and Stage 2 are mirror images on this 2×2. Each is good at exactly the task type the other is bad at. Neither dominates the other globally.

05 / The η_init U-curve

The architectural cost is irreducible by prior tuning

The most obvious response to "Stage 2 loses on MQAR" is "the prior is wrong — raise η_init." A 5-point sweep tested this directly on hard MQAR, varying b_η to set η_init ∈ {0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 0.8, 0.99}:

η_init b_η sat step final acc plateau structure
0.1 (canonical) −2.197 375 0.9806late entry, short plateau
0.3 −0.847 400 0.9708early entry, long plateau
0.5 0.000 475 0.9977early entry, very long plateau
0.8 1.386 425 0.9990mid entry, quick grok
0.994.5953250.9995fastest of sweep
sat 500 | * (η=0.5, 475) | * (η=0.8, 425) 450 | * 400 | * (η=0.3) | * (η=0.1, 375) 375 | 350 | 325 | * (η=0.99, 325) 300 | 250 | ─── Stage 1 (250) ─────────────────────── 225 | ─── baseline (225) ────────────────────── +──┬─────┬─────┬─────┬─────┬──── η_init 0.1 0.3 0.5 0.8 0.99

The landscape is non-monotonic with a wide trough in the middle. The worst point is η = 0.5 (sat = 475); both extremes (0.1 and 0.99) escape faster. The best Stage 2 prior — η = 0.99, which mimics Stage 1's α = η = 1 starting condition — still trails baseline by 100 steps and Stage 1 by 75 steps.

Why the middle is worst

MQAR has a wide partial-recall basin at ~50% accuracy — a solution where the model can answer half the queries via partial use of memory + attention. Whether the model gets stuck in that basin depends on η_init:

  • η = 0.1 (canonical): memory engages too late to enter the basin during the basin's high-gradient phase. Model learns full-recall directly. Late entry, short plateau.
  • η = 0.3, 0.5 (moderate): memory engages early into the basin's most attractive region. Model gets stuck refining the partial solution. Long plateau (> 250 steps).
  • η = 0.99 (extreme): memory writes are at full strength from step 0. The partial solution is dominated by the full solution from the start; the basin is no longer competitive. Mid entry, short plateau, fastest grok.

This is a property of the loss landscape × η_init interaction, not the architecture per se. But it explains why "just tune the prior" doesn't work: the prior choice doesn't change the irreducible cost of having learnable gates, it only navigates the local basin structure differently.

The ~75-100 step irreducible cost

Even at η_init = 0.99 — where Stage 2's initial conditions are essentially identical to Stage 1's α = η = 1 — Stage 2 still trails Stage 1 by 75 steps. The architecture pays for:

  1. Gate-parameter optimization budget. Stage 1 has no W_α, W_η, b_α, b_η to update; the gates are constants. Stage 2 has to allocate gradient signal to those four matrices/vectors. That's pure optimization cost regardless of where the gates start.
  2. Inductive bias of "selectivity is possible." Even if the gates stay near 1.0, the model knows the gates can deviate. This widens the effective hypothesis space the optimizer searches in early training. On a uniform-write task, this extra freedom is pure cost.

Tuning the prior — moving η_init from 0.1 to 0.99 — covers ~50 steps of the 150-step Stage-2-vs-baseline gap on MQAR. The remaining ~75-100 steps are the architectural floor.

06 / The δ-mem connection

Same axis, different mechanism

The bench result reproduces the δ-mem field-result axis at probe scale:

Mechanism Selective state tracking Uniform recall
δ-mem (rank-8 recurrent state with delta-rule updates, learned gates) lifts (HumanEval +12.2pp, MBPP +8.0pp, GSM8K +5.5pp) null (HotpotQA ΔF1 = +0.0003)
Stage 2 (additive linear-attention memory, learned per-token α/η) lifts (SC sat 100 vs baseline 200) loses (MQAR sat 375 vs baseline 225)

Two completely different architectural mechanisms, identical axis-of-value. The thing they share is learned selectivity at the per-token level: both have parameters that decide, conditional on the input token, how much to write into and read out of memory state. Fixed-gate architectures (Stage 1) don't have this knob; pure-attention architectures (baseline) don't have memory state at all.

The bench cleanly distinguishes the two regimes that the δ-mem field result identified empirically. Stage 2 is what δ-mem looks like at small scale on synthetic probes: it wins where selective gating matters, is irrelevant or actively harmful otherwise.

07 / Implications for the Stage 2 verdict

The "neutral" aggregate had structure

The 2026-05-05 Hope/NL track synthesis concluded: at d6/5000-iter on ClimbMix with a MMLU/GSM8K SFT mix, Stage 2 is neutral, not net-positive, on natural-language val_bpb. That conclusion stands on what it measured. The bench result adds the missing internal structure:

  1. Stage 2 has an architectural signature net-positive on selective state-tracking tasks that LM val_bpb doesn't isolate. SC at d4 saturates 2× faster than baseline.
  2. Stage 2 has an architectural signature net-negative on uniform-recall tasks that LM val_bpb also doesn't isolate. MQAR at d4 saturates 1.67× slower than baseline; no η_init prior closes the gap.
  3. The two effects net out on mixed natural-language pretraining. "Neutral" is the correct aggregate. It hides a real internal structure where the architecture has wins and losses on opposite task axes.

Future Stage 2 evaluations should:

  • Test on selective-state-heavy benchmarks (code generation, multi-step reasoning, instruction following). Predicted: Stage 2 wins.
  • Test on uniform-recall-heavy benchmarks (open-domain QA over long contexts, knowledge retrieval). Predicted: Stage 2 loses or is neutral.
  • Not tune η_init globally. The U-curve says there's no universally-optimal prior; the optimum is task-mix-dependent. Either accept canonical defaults and document the task-mix sensitivity, or make η_init input-conditional (e.g., a small MLP on global input statistics — out of scope for v0).

This is a sharper architectural picture than "Stage 2 is neutral, defer." The original verdict's aggregate conclusion is unchanged; the mechanism is now explained.

08 / What's left

The next-level questions

Three priorities, in increasing order of cost:

  1. n=2-3 seeds on the four headline numbers. Stage 2 SC sat=100, Stage 2 MQAR sat=375 (η=0.1), Stage 2 MQAR sat=475 (η=0.5, the U-curve worst), Stage 1 SC sat=375. Cheapest path to bounding the seed-noise component of every claim. ~2 h wall.
  2. η_init sweep on hard SC. Mirror of the MQAR U-curve. Tests whether SC's win at η=0.1 is fragile to prior choice (if SC also has a U-shape with η=0.1 happening to land on the good side) or structural (if SC is robustly good across the η range). ~100 min wall. The directly motivated follow-up.
  3. Stage 2 at d6 on hard MQAR + SC. Bridges back to the LM arc. If the d6 results reproduce the d4 pattern, the original LM "neutral" verdict gets its mechanism explanation at the architecture's native scale. ~50 min per task per arm = 200 min total.

Lower-priority follow-ups: learnable η_init via a small MLP on input statistics (tests whether the task-mix problem dissolves with input-conditional priors); induction-heads probe (adds a third axis between recall and state-tracking); architectural variants — swap topology vs additive, different memory layer placements.

The 2026-05-13 strategic-pivot decision stands: the d6/d8 from-scratch LM arc is paused; bench is where memory-mechanism iteration happens now. The bench v0 milestone closes with the architecture having a documented task signature instead of an opaque "neutral." That's the v0 product.

10 / Coda

The aggregate hid the architecture

Two weeks ago we wrote: "Stage 2 is neutral, not net-positive, on natural-language val_bpb. The MQAR probe shows the mechanism works on synthetic recall; the corpus + horizon don't reward it on natural text." Both clauses were correct. The second clause, in particular, was a careful hedge: this corpus, this horizon, this scale. We didn't claim the architecture was bad — only that we couldn't find it doing useful work.

What the bench surfaces is that the architecture was doing useful work the whole time. It just wasn't the kind of work that aggregated to a visible LM-loss delta. Stage 2's learned gates are net-positive on selective state-tracking tasks and net-negative on uniform-recall tasks. Mixed natural-language pretraining stresses both; the two effects cancel; val_bpb says zero. The original conclusion is correct on what it measured and silent on what it didn't.

The methodological lesson generalizes beyond Stage 2. LM-loss aggregates can hide architectural structure that synthetic probes will reveal in minutes. The d6/d8 LM-loss arc cost roughly 30 h of M2 wall to produce "neutral, defer." The bench cost ~2.5 h to produce "wins on selective tasks, loses on uniform tasks, the prior tuning is non-monotonic, and the architectural floor is irreducible." Different questions, different tools — the bench was the right tool for the question we couldn't get at with LM val_bpb.

The δ-mem detour pointed at the right axis (selective state vs uniform recall); the bench reproduced it on our own architecture; the η_init sweep ruled out the obvious "just tune the prior" escape. Stage 2 is neither dead nor a sleeping win — it's a tool with a known task signature. The Hope/NL track is back online, oriented to a more concrete question than the LM-loss arc could pose: does the selective-state advantage hold at d6 and translate to natural-text benchmarks that stress it?
