From four closed levers to a coarse-codebook bet

A one-day arc on d6 chat quality, ending in a categorical diagnostic that points at Bonsai-LoRA.

nanochat · experiment/hope-nested-learning · 2026-05-06

We started the day reviewing whether the Hope/NL synthesis verdict had measured the right thing. By evening we had closed four levers on d6 chat quality (architecture, decoding, SFT-rebalance, SFT-additive), produced our cleanest evidence yet that SFT val_bpb is not predictive of chat quality, and — via a sibling project's two-axis debug framework — categorized d6's failure modes mechanistically. The diagnostic says ~65 % of d6's chat failures are FP-flavored right-context drift, which has a known fix direction. That fix points squarely at Bonsai-LoRA, which a session earlier was just one of several speculative directions.

1. The reframe — from architecture to chat quality

Yesterday's Hope/NL synthesis closed the architectural track at d6 with the verdict "Stage 2 is neutral, not net-positive, on natural-language val_bpb." That verdict was technically correct on the metric used, but the metric had a known weakness — Stage 1.5's probe-design doc explicitly said:

LM val_bpb on DCLM/SFT mixtures is a sum across token positions; positions where carrying state matters are a small fraction of the total. A 1 % val_bpb gap can be either real architectural divergence on the load-bearing positions or noise on the bulk.

We pivoted to the user's reframing: "Provably useful continuous learning in an LLM, in a harness that works on my laptop. The chatbot is kinda bad, especially after turn 1. What can we do without inflating wall-clock or buying M4?"

The reframe changed which task class we were evaluating on. Free multi-turn chat coherence is exactly the regime where recurrent memory mechanisms should differentially help if they help anywhere — within a single sequence, accumulating context across turns, exactly the regime the synthesis verdict had skipped.

Bias-controlling move. Pre-registered a 7-prompt multi-turn rubric with per-prompt pass/fail criteria before running anything. Each prompt was designed to exercise a specific memory-load-bearing pattern: persona retention, reference resolution, numerical thread, topic stickiness, constraint accumulation, self-correction, open drift. Prompts and bar were committed in af2d86a; results landed in 5008fe2.

2. Closing four levers

Over four experiments we tested the four interventions available without retraining the base model from scratch or buying new compute. Each was scored on the same 7-prompt rubric.

2.1 Architecture (Stage 2 vs baseline)

Multi-turn A/B between d6_baseline_modern_sft (vanilla) and d6_stage2_pretrain_s1_sft (Hope/NL Stage 2 with learned-gate memory at L3). Decoding held constant. Both passed exactly 1 of 7 prompts (loose pass on open_drift, where both models maintained their T1 theme to T5). Same failure modes, same magnitudes — the architectural delta on user-relevant chat coherence was not measurable.

2.2 Decoding (5-config sweep)

Same 7-prompt rubric, baseline only, five decoding configurations: default, greedy, rep_penalty=1.2, rep_penalty=1.4, fake-system-prompt-prefix. Range of scores: 0/7 to 1.5/7. The best config (greedy) wins one prompt fully because greedy preserves user-injected facts verbatim ("Canberra, not Sydney" → repeated correctly at T4). All other configs ≤ 1/7. Repetition penalty trades repetition for coherence with no sweet spot. Fake system-prompt backfires into self-referential identity loops.

2.3 SFT-rebalance (Variant A: cut MMLU/GSM8K)

Reduced --mmlu-epochs from 3 → 1 and --gsm8k-epochs from 4 → 1. Structured-task share dropped from 57 % → 46 %. val_bpb improved by 0.053 absolute (0.6483 → 0.5952; ~30× the seed-noise spread we measured on A2). Multi-turn rubric regressed by 1 (1/7 → 0/7).

2.4 SFT-additive (Variant B1: 2 × SmolTalk)

Added a --smoltalk-epochs flag, doubled SmolTalk to bring chat-share from 43 % → 60 %. Mmlu/gsm8k unchanged (isolates the chat-volume variable). val_bpb 0.6027 (between original and chatmix_a). Multi-turn rubric 0/7, same as Variant A.

3. The cleanest finding — val_bpb ⊥ chat quality

Same intervention (chatmix_a vs baseline) pushed val_bpb in one direction by an enormous margin and the multi-turn rubric in the opposite direction. 0.053 bpb of improvement on the held-out SFT mix bought us a full prompt of regression on multi-turn chat. By every loss-based metric we'd ever pre-register, chatmix_a is the better model. The user-relevant task says it's worse.

This is the single durable thing we proved today, regardless of what comes next: SFT val_bpb does not predict chat quality at d6 scale. The two metrics aren't just decoupled — they can diverge sharply on the same model under the same intervention.

3.1 What's actually happening in the transcripts

Cutting MMLU and GSM8K epochs didn't eliminate template-locking. It shuffled which templates dominate on ambiguous inputs:

  • Identity-loop became dominant. The CustomJSON identity examples (1 K rows × 2 epochs, 0.2 % of mix unchanged) gained relative salience because the structured tasks shrank around them. chatmix_a immediately replied "I'm nanochat, a Large Language Model. I beat GPT-2's CORE benchmark, 0.25851..." to "Hi I'm Alex." Original baseline gave a generic SmolTalk reply.
  • SpellingBee leaked into chat. Mix share rose 7 % → 9.4 %, enough for "find the number 'a' in 'far Sydney': f, a, r, k, i, a..." to trigger on text-heavy ambiguous input.
  • GSM8K template not eliminated. 1 % mix-share is still enough for <|python_start|>...<|output_start|>...####N to fire on number-shaped input. Reducing volume reduces frequency, not trigger condition.
  • New degenerate suffix loops appeared. "Portland-y-y-y-y-..." and "the Red Delux, the Red Delux, ..." — pure repetition where template coverage dropped.

smoltalk_2x landed in a third distinct profile: "Java J. I'm working on Java J." for persona retention (got "software engineer" right but invented "J" as the name and looped); "too too too too..." pure repetition; "Sydney Sydney Sydney..." for capital-of-Australia.

The pattern, three runs in: the model has finite template-learning capacity at 74 M. Rebalancing within existing data redistributes which templates win the salience battle; it doesn't free capacity for less template-locked free chat. Every rebalance produces differently broken, not less broken.

4. External vs internal memory — and the productive dichotomy

The post-experiment review surfaced two different directions for "continuous learning at laptop scale" that today's session had implicitly touched but not made explicit:

External-loop memory: Feedback Descent

Maintain a "current best" artifact; iteratively mutate via pairwise comparisons paired with textual rationales; keep the rationales as accumulated context history. No weight updates anywhere.

The Lee/Boen/Finn paper (arXiv 2511.07919) shows pairwise feedback with explanatory rationale — kept as context history rather than collapsed to a binary preference — gives dimension-free convergence in semantic space. They beat specialized molecular optimizers using only text, surpassing the 99.9 % percentile of a 260K-compound database.

The mechanistic insight: scalar reward → zeroth-order → exponential slowdown with effective dimension; textual feedback → first-order analog → dimension-free convergence. The information bottleneck framing maps directly onto today's val_bpb / multi-turn divergence: we were collapsing rich judgment into a per-token average.

Internal-weight memory: LoRA

Tiny rank-r adapter matrices riding on top of the frozen base. ~0.1 % of base parameters trainable. Modular — swap or compose at inference time.

Karpathy's nanochat ships no PEFT path; full-parameter SFT and full-parameter RL are the only training routes. For the laptop / continuous-learning use case this is a real gap. LoRA maps onto the operator's stated goal more cleanly than either Hope/NL architecture (which we just closed) or FD (which is purely inference-time, no adaptation).

The compose-with-FD bridge: an FD loop generates a trajectory of (input, refined_output) pairs; LoRA-fine-tune on the trajectory; the adapter internalizes the iteration as a one-shot forward pass. External loop ↔ internal architecture, with LoRA as the bridge between them. Nobody has run this end-to-end at small scale that we're aware of.

Two proposals committed (92eb7fe + 0ff3018): five FD directions, three LoRA directions, with explicit bridges between them.

5. Phase 5 + the cosine-NN diagnostic

The trx4mr sibling project's Phase 5 retro arrived as we were sketching proposals. Several findings echoed today's session immediately, but the most actionable was their two-axis debug framework:

For practitioners debugging "why is this token's embedding weird," the two-axis framework is a quick first pass. Compute the cosine NN of the misbehaving token's embedding. If the NN is a token with similar next-token distributions, you're in the right-context regime (FP-style drift). If the NN is a token that appears in similar positions / predecessors, you're in the left-context regime (binary-style coarseness collision). Different fixes for each.
failure modediagnosticfix direction
right-context drift (FP-flavored) NN shares next-token distribution Coarsen codebook (binary/ternary) OR add capacity
left-context collision (binary-flavored) NN shares preceding-context distribution Add codebook levels (ternary) OR increase d_model

Three lines of numpy. We had the failures already documented. The diagnostic was free.

5.2 The mechanism for the math-mode reflex

This was the most striking single result in the probe output. <|python_start|>'s top-10 nearest neighbors in embedding space: 

'Ma' (0.37)  ',['  (0.30)  ' YOU' (0.28)  '(f' (0.28)  '['  (0.28)
' (+' (0.27)  '/('  (0.27)  ' ``' (0.27)  ' `' (0.26)  ' €'  (0.26)

These are bracket / parens / syntax tokens. The special token has been embedded — through SFT on GSM8K solutions — as "the thing that comes right before bracket-prefixed Python syntax." When the model is in any state semantically near a digit (which clusters with other digits, which cluster with this special token via co-occurrence in the GSM8K template), it generates <|python_start|> with high probability.

Implication: the math-mode reflex isn't about how much GSM8K we trained on. We tried 1 epoch (chatmix_a) and it still fired. The trigger is the embedding geometry, which is stable across mix-share rebalances. Cutting GSM8K changes the firing frequency but not the trigger condition.

5.3 The Sydney attractor

'Sydney''s cluster is densely populated with other places (China, Georgia, Utah, Malaysia, London, Ontario, Pakistan...). In a model state where the predicted next-token distribution is "capital cities," Sydney is one of the most strongly-attracted tokens; once selected, the next-token state stays in "cities," cycling back to Sydney. Right-context drift creates a self-reinforcing attractor.

This explains "Sydney is the capital of Sydney" and "Sydney Sydney Sydney Sydney..." mechanically. The user telling the model "Canberra, not Sydney" doesn't alter the embedding geometry; once Sydney's attractor is engaged, the model returns to it.

6. Where we land

Per the Phase 5 fix-direction table, ~65 % right-context drift is in the regime where codebook coarsening (binary/ternary STE) is the indicated fix; ~18 % left-context collision is in the regime where capacity (M4 / d8+) is the fix. Both interventions are warranted, but in those proportions.

Mapping onto the resources we have:

┌────────────────────────────┐ ┌────────────────────────────┐ │ d6 fp baseline │ │ M4 / d8+ extension │ │ (today's status) │ ──────> │ (capacity for ~18% of │ │ - 4 levers closed │ │ failures; secondary) │ │ - dominant-axis FP drift │ │ ~6-9h Phase 0 + 11-17h │ └────────────────────────────┘ └────────────────────────────┘ │ │ binary base + fp adapter ▼ ┌────────────────────────────┐ │ Bonsai-LoRA │ │ (codebook coarseness + │ │ fp adaptation) │ │ ~3-5h L1 + ~1d Bonsai infra│ │ diagnostic-supported │ └────────────────────────────┘

Bonsai's 1-bit base provides codebook coarsening by construction; fp LoRA on top adapts cheaply without disturbing base coarseness. Per the diagnostic, this is the indicated fix for the dominant failure axis. It's also the only one that fits our compute budget without buying new hardware.

Resource economics, stated honestly. STE-based binary training on M2 doesn't save resources — latent fp32 weights stay in memory, the quantize op adds work in the forward pass, MPS kernels for grouped-quantize aren't optimized. Native 1-bit recipes are designed for big-compute targets. The path to access Phase 5's regime-robustness benefits at our scale is to not retrain binary; it's to use Bonsai (which already paid the QAT cost on bigger hardware) and add small fp LoRA adapters.

6.2 What the day's verdict is, stated tightly

At d6 / 74M, with available data and current-architecture training, the chatbot's quality is bounded by capacity and template-locking. Within that ceiling, the failure modes are predominantly FP-flavored right-context drift, with a secondary capacity-bound left-context-collision component. Codebook coarsening (Bonsai-LoRA) is the diagnostic- supported indicated fix for the dominant axis; M4 is the indicated fix for the secondary axis. Both warranted, in those proportions. Today's session closed every full-parameter full-precision lever; the open paths now sit specifically in those two regimes, and they're both small enough to fit our budget if we choose them.
